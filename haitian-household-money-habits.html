5 Money Habits Passed Down in Haitian Households — And Which Ones to Keep

5 Abitid Lajan Pase Desann nan Kay Ayisyen — Ak Ki Sa Pou Kenbe
← Back to Blog ← Retounen nan Blòg
CommunityKominote·July 2026

Every community carries its own unwritten rules about money — habits passed from parent to child, often without ever being explicitly taught. In Haitian households, some of these traditions are genuine financial superpowers. Others might quietly be holding families back. Here's an honest look at five of the most common ones.

1. The "Sòl" Savings Circle

Keep it. A sòl (rotating savings group) builds discipline and community trust in a way a bank account alone doesn't. The main risk is relying on it as your only savings strategy — pair it with a personal emergency fund too.

2. Sending Money Home Every Month, No Matter What

Keep it, but budget for it. Supporting family abroad is a value, not a flaw. The habit worth building is treating remittances as a planned line item — not an afterthought that eats into savings unpredictably.

3. Avoiding Banks and Keeping Cash at Home

Worth rethinking. This habit often comes from real historical mistrust, but cash at home earns no interest and isn't protected if something happens. A basic, FDIC-insured bank account is one of the simplest upgrades a family can make.

4. Investing Everything in Property Back Home

Balance it. Real estate abroad can be meaningful, but concentrating all your savings in one asset in another country carries real risk. Diversifying — even modestly — with insurance, retirement savings, or a strategy like Infinite Banking here in the U.S. adds stability.

5. Not Talking About Money With Kids

Worth rethinking. Many of us grew up not knowing our family's finances at all. Teaching kids the basics — budgeting, saving, credit — early gives the next generation a head start that many of us didn't get.

The Bigger Picture

None of these habits exist in isolation — they come from resilience, sacrifice, and love for family. The goal isn't to abandon what our community has built, but to add a few modern tools alongside it, so that resilience turns into lasting wealth for the next generation too.

Chak kominote gen pwòp règ ekri sou lajan — abitid ki pase de paran a pitit, souvan san yo pa janm anseye eksplisitman. Nan kay Ayisyen, kèk nan tradisyon sa yo se vrè superpouvwa finansye. Lòt yo ka an silans ap kenbe fanmi yo dèyè. Men yon gade onèt sou senk nan pi komen yo.

1. Sèk Epay "Sòl" La

Kenbe li. Yon sòl (sèk epay wotasyon) bati disiplin ak konfyans kominote yon fason yon kont bank sèlman pa fè. Pi gwo risk lan se depann sou li kòm sèl estrateji epay ou — konbine li ak yon fon ijans pèsonèl tou.

2. Voye Lajan Lakay Chak Mwa, Kèlkeswa Sa

Kenbe li, men fè bidjè pou li. Soutni fanmi aletranje se yon valè, se pa yon defo. Abitid ki vo lapèn bati a se trete lajan voye yo kòm yon liy planifye — pa yon apre-panse ki manje nan epay san planifikasyon.

3. Evite Bank Yo Epi Kenbe Lajan Kach Lakay

Vo lapèn repanse. Abitid sa a souvan soti nan yon vrè mefyans istorik, men lajan kach lakay pa touche okenn enterè epi li pa pwoteje si yon bagay rive. Yon kont bank debaz, ki asire pa FDIC, se youn nan mizajou ki pi senp yon fanmi ka fè.

4. Envesti Tout Bagay nan Pwopriyete Lakay

Balanse li. Imobilye aletranje ka gen sans, men konsantre tout epay ou nan yon sèl byen nan yon lòt peyi pote vrè risk. Divèsifye — menm modèsteman — ak asirans, epay retrèt, oswa yon estrateji tankou Bank Enfini isit Etazini ajoute estabilite.

5. Pa Pale Sou Lajan Ak Timoun Yo

Vo lapèn repanse. Anpil nan nou grandi san nou pa konnen finans fanmi nou ditou. Anseye timoun yo bazik yo — bidjè, epay, kredi — bonè bay pwochen jenerasyon an yon depa avans anpil nan nou pa t genyen.
