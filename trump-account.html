The Trump Account: What Every Parent Needs to Know Right Now

✍️ Miguelson Etienne, Professional Tax Specialist ⏱️ 8 min read Savings · Tax · Family Finance

The U.S. government just created a brand-new savings account for children — and if your baby was born in 2025 or later, they qualify for a free $1,000 deposit. This is not a rumor. It's real, it's federal law, and it's called the Trump Account.

As someone who helps families navigate insurance, taxes, and financial planning, I want to make sure every parent in our community understands this opportunity before it slips by. Let me break it down clearly — no jargon, no confusion.

The Bottom Line

The Trump Account is a new tax-deferred investment account for children under 18, created by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed July 2025). Kids born 2025–2028 get a free $1,000 from the government. Parents can add up to $5,000 per year, and the money grows tax-deferred until the child turns 18.

What Exactly Is the Trump Account?

Officially called a 530A IRA, the Trump Account is a federally backed savings and investment account designed to help American families build long-term wealth starting from birth. It works similarly to a Traditional IRA — but it's specifically designed for children who have no earned income yet.

The account was created as part of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed into law by President Trump in July 2025. It represents one of the most significant changes to children's savings policy in decades.

Who Qualifies?

Here is who can open a Trump Account and who qualifies for the $1,000 government deposit:

  • Children born January 1, 2025 – December 31, 2028 → eligible for the free $1,000 government deposit
  • Any child under 18 with a valid Social Security number can have a Trump Account — even if born before 2025
  • No income limit for families — any household can open one regardless of how much they earn
  • One account per child — you cannot open multiple accounts for the same child
  • Children born before 2025 who are under age 10 and live in qualifying ZIP codes may receive a $250 donation from the Dell Foundation (details below)

Dell Foundation Bonus

The Michael & Susan Dell Foundation pledged $6.25 billion to fund $250 deposits for up to 25 million children age 10 and under, born before 2025, who live in ZIP codes with a median household income under $150,000. Many families in Central Florida qualify for this — check at trumpaccounts.gov.

How Much Can You Contribute?

Beyond the government's $1,000 gift, here's what you and your family can add:

  • Parents, grandparents, family members: up to a combined $5,000 per year per child
  • Employers (yours or the child's): up to $2,500 per year — this counts toward the $5,000 limit
  • Government entities and charities: can contribute tax-free without counting toward the annual limit
  • The $1,000 government seed deposit does NOT count toward the $5,000 annual limit
  • Contributions are in after-tax dollars (not tax-deductible, but growth is tax-deferred)
  • No earned income requirement — anyone can contribute on behalf of the child

How Is the Money Invested?

The law is specific about this: Trump Account funds must be invested in low-cost U.S. stock index funds or ETFs that:

  • Track the S&P 500 or another major U.S. equity index
  • Do not use leverage
  • Have annual fees of no more than 0.10%

You cannot pick individual stocks or active funds. This keeps costs low and ensures the money is working in the market over the long term.

What Could It Be Worth at Age 18?

Based on the historical average return of the S&P 500 (~7% per year after inflation):

  • The $1,000 government deposit alone, invested for 18 years at 7%, grows to roughly $3,400
  • If parents contribute the maximum $5,000/year for 18 years at 7%, the total could reach $180,000–$200,000+
  • Even modest contributions of $100/month for 18 years at 7% could grow to approximately $48,000

Note: These are estimates based on historical returns. Past performance does not guarantee future results.

What Happens When the Child Turns 18?

At age 18, the Trump Account converts to a traditional IRA under the child's name and full control. Key rules:

  • From age 18 on, only the child can contribute (no more parental contributions)
  • The child must have earned income to continue contributing after 18
  • Withdrawals before age 18 are not allowed under any circumstances
  • After 18, regular IRA distribution rules apply (taxes and potential penalties on early withdrawal)

How to Open a Trump Account — Step by Step

1

Get your child's Social Security Number

You must have this before you can open the account. If your newborn doesn't have one yet, apply at the hospital or your local SSA office.

2

File IRS Form 4547

This is the official form to elect your child for a Trump Account. You can file it with your 2025 tax return, or submit it independently through the IRS Trump Accounts portal. Filing with your 2025 return is recommended to ensure your account is ready as soon as contributions open.

3

Or use the online portal

Starting July 2026, you can also set up the account at trumpaccounts.gov. This is the most direct way once the portal is live.

4

Start contributing (July 4, 2026)

Contributions to Trump Accounts officially open on July 4, 2026. The $1,000 government deposit for eligible newborns will also be funded at this time.

5

Consider pairing with other strategies

A Trump Account works well alongside a 529 plan (for education), a Roth IRA (once the child has earned income), and — for families interested in building cash value — an Infinite Banking Concept (IBC) whole life policy.
