How Tax Brackets Actually Work in 2026 — With Real Examples for Every Filing Status

Kijan Pal Taks Yo Fonksyone Vrèman an 2026 — Ak Egzanp Reyèl pou Chak Estati Depo
← Back to Blog ← Retounen nan Blòg
Tax TipsKonsèy Taks·November 2026

If you've ever heard someone say "I don't want a raise, it'll push me into a higher tax bracket and I'll take home less," here's good news: that's not how U.S. taxes work. Nobody loses money by earning more. This article breaks down exactly how the 2026 federal tax brackets work, with real worked examples for single, married, and head-of-household filers — plus how your state of residence changes the picture in Florida, Massachusetts, and New Jersey, the three states where we're licensed to help with insurance.

Marginal vs. Effective Tax Rate — The Idea Everyone Mixes Up

The U.S. uses a progressive, marginal tax system. Your income is split into layers ("brackets"), and each layer is taxed at its own rate — only the portion of income that falls inside a bracket is taxed at that bracket's rate. Your marginal rate is the rate on your last dollar earned. Your effective rate is your total tax divided by your total income, and it's almost always lower than your marginal rate, because the lower brackets are taxed at lower rates first.

Think of it like filling buckets: the first bucket (10%) has to be completely full before any income spills into the 12% bucket, and so on. Earning more money never reduces what lands in your pocket — it just means the next dollars are taxed a little more.

Worked Example 1: Single Filer, $60,000 Income

Gross income of $60,000, minus the single standard deduction of $16,100, leaves $43,900 of taxable income.

  • 10% on the first $12,400 = $1,240
  • 12% on the next $31,500 (from $12,400 to $43,900) = $3,780

Total federal tax: $5,020 — an effective rate of about 8.4% on gross income, even though this filer's marginal rate is 12%.

Worked Example 2: Married Filing Jointly, $120,000 Income

Gross income of $120,000, minus the joint standard deduction of $32,200, leaves $87,800 of taxable income.

  • 10% on the first $24,800 = $2,480
  • 12% on the next $63,000 (from $24,800 to $87,800) = $7,560

Total federal tax: $10,040 — the same 12% marginal bracket as the single filer above. Because the married brackets are roughly double-width, a couple earning twice as much pays almost exactly the same effective rate, about 8.4%.

Worked Example 3: Head of Household, $75,000 Income

Gross income of $75,000, minus the head-of-household standard deduction of $24,150, leaves $50,850 of taxable income.

  • 10% on the first $17,700 = $1,770
  • 12% on the next $33,150 (from $17,700 to $50,850) = $3,978

Total federal tax: $5,748 — an effective rate of about 7.7%. Head-of-household brackets sit between single and joint brackets, which is one advantage of qualifying for this filing status when you're unmarried and supporting a dependent.

Worked Example 4: Single Filer, $150,000 Income (Reaching the 24% Bracket)

Gross income of $150,000, minus the $16,100 standard deduction, leaves $133,900 of taxable income — enough to reach into the 24% bracket.

  • 10% on the first $12,400 = $1,240
  • 12% on the next $38,000 (up to $50,400) = $4,560
  • 22% on the next $55,300 (up to $105,700) = $12,166
  • 24% on the remaining $28,200 (up to $133,900) = $6,768

Total federal tax: $24,734 — an effective rate of about 16.5%, well below the 24% marginal rate. Only the last $28,200 of income is actually taxed at 24%; every dollar before that was taxed at a lower rate.

Now Add Your State: Florida vs. Massachusetts vs. New Jersey

Everything above is federal tax — the same no matter where you live. ME Shield is licensed to help with insurance in three very different tax states, so where you live changes your total picture significantly:

StateHow it taxes incomeTop rate
FloridaNo state income tax at all0%
MassachusettsFlat 5% on most income, plus an additional 4% "millionaire's surtax" on income above roughly $1.1 million (the exact threshold is adjusted for inflation each year)9% (5% + 4% surtax)
New JerseyProgressive, seven brackets from 1.4% up to 10.75%; the top rate applies to income over $1 million regardless of filing status10.75%

In practice: the single filer earning $150,000 in Example 4 above would owe $0 in state income tax in Florida, roughly $7,500 more (at the flat 5% rate) if that income were earned in Massachusetts, and an amount somewhere in New Jersey's lower-to-middle brackets — a few percent of income, well under the 10.75% top rate reserved for income over $1 million — if earned there.

This is one reason "how much should I plan for" never has a one-size-fits-all answer: your state of residence matters just as much as your filing status and income level.

To Maginal kont To Efektif — Lide Tout Moun Konfonn

Ozetazini itilize yon sistèm taks pwogresif ak maginal. Revni ou divize an kouch ("pal"), epi chak kouch taksab a pwòp to pa li — se sèlman pòsyon revni ki tonbe anndan yon pal ki taksab nan to pal sa a. To maginal ou se to sou dènye dola ou touche. To efektif ou se total taks ou divize pa total revni ou, epi li prèske toujou pi ba pase to maginal ou, paske pal ki pi ba yo taksab nan to ki pi ba yo an premye.

Panse a sa tankou ranpli bokit: premye bokit la (10%) dwe ranpli nèt anvan okenn revni debòde nan bokit 12% la, epi konsa de swit. Touche plis lajan pa janm diminye sa ki rive nan pòch ou — li jis vle di pwochen dola yo taksab yon ti kras plis.

Egzanp Konkrè 1: Moun Sèl, Revni $60,000

Revni brit $60,000, mwens dediksyon estanda sèl $16,100, kite $43,900 revni taksab.

  • 10% sou premye $12,400 = $1,240
  • 12% sou pwochen $31,500 (soti nan $12,400 rive $43,900) = $3,780

Total taks federal: $5,020 — yon to efektif anviwon 8.4% sou revni brit la, menm si to maginal moun sa a se 12%.

Egzanp Konkrè 2: Marye Depo Ansanm, Revni $120,000

Revni brit $120,000, mwens dediksyon estanda ansanm $32,200, kite $87,800 revni taksab.

  • 10% sou premye $24,800 = $2,480
  • 12% sou pwochen $63,000 (soti nan $24,800 rive $87,800) = $7,560

Total taks federal: $10,040 — menm pal maginal 12% ak moun sèl ki anwo a. Paske pal marye yo apeprè double lajè, yon koup ki touche de fwa plis peye prèske menm to efektif la, anviwon 8.4%.

Egzanp Konkrè 3: Chèf Kay, Revni $75,000

Revni brit $75,000, mwens dediksyon estanda chèf kay $24,150, kite $50,850 revni taksab.

  • 10% sou premye $17,700 = $1,770
  • 12% sou pwochen $33,150 (soti nan $17,700 rive $50,850) = $3,978

Total taks federal: $5,748 — yon to efektif anviwon 7.7%. Pal chèf kay yo chita ant pal sèl ak pal ansanm yo, ki se youn nan avantaj pou kalifye pou estati sa a lè ou pa marye epi w ap sipòte yon depandan.

Egzanp Konkrè 4: Moun Sèl, Revni $150,000 (Rive nan Pal 24%)

Revni brit $150,000, mwens dediksyon estanda $16,100, kite $133,900 revni taksab — ase pou rive nan pal 24% la.

  • 10% sou premye $12,400 = $1,240
  • 12% sou pwochen $38,000 (rive $50,400) = $4,560
  • 22% sou pwochen $55,300 (rive $105,700) = $12,166
  • 24% sou res $28,200 (rive $133,900) = $6,768

Total taks federal: $24,734 — yon to efektif anviwon 16.5%, byen anba to maginal 24% la. Se sèlman dènye $28,200 revni a ki vrèman taksab nan 24%; chak dola anvan sa te taksab nan yon to ki pi ba.
